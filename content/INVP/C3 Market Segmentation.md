Market segmentation is about looking at what the **customer** wants.

## Types of Market Segments
1. **Demographic**
   divides according to population, age, gender etc.
2. **Geographic**
   according to location
3. **Psychographic**
   focuses on the individual psychology; whether a salesperson can convince you
4. **Behavioural**
   Analyse customer's behaviour and target them based off it.
   Customers like to grab a coffee on the road, so company opens a coffee shop with drive-through service.
## Pain Point
- **pain point:** A problem that bothers customers enough that they would be willing to pay for your solution.
- 4 Types:
	- **Financial**
	  Not enough money... (just like me fr)
	- **Productivity**
	  Unhappy with the quality of the product.
	- **Process**
	- **Support**
	  Customer service is bad.

To get started, discover a customer’s **pain point** that can be solved with an idea/product. 

>[!Example] Watermelon Lover
>Imagine your ideal customer is a watermelon lover, but he doesn’t like having *sticky fingers* after cutting the watermelon. Or, even worse, he doesn’t like to *spend the time* to slice through the thick skin. 
>
>This is a pain point — a specific issue that drives customer nuts  and is as diverse and varied as your prospective customer.
>
>Sometimes, just acknowledging a pain point can lead to an entirely new product. Hence, you want to position it as a solution to their problem to make them happy.
>So, in this case, a giant watermelon slicer.
## Beachhead Market
- **beachhead market:** the first narrowly defined customer segment a business focuses on winning.
- named after the WWII battle of Normandy where Allied soldiers stormed the beachheads of Normandy enabling them to dominate one of the most important battles of WWII
  ~~damn dramatic much?~~
- Selected after evaluating target customers spending power, willingness to purchase the product, intensity of competition, company’s capabilities, scalability potential etc.

Start by exploring opportunities in multiple markets and then narrow down to a *single* beachhead market. Conduct [[#Primary Research|primary markey research]] on potential markets, the findings will help you choose.

> “A beachhead market is the place where, once you gain a dominant market share, you will have the strength to attack adjacent markets with different opportunities, building a larger company with each new following,” says Aulet.

### 3 Conditions of a Beachhead market
1. The customers within the market all ==buy similar products==.
2. The customers within the market have a ==similar sales cycle== and expect products to provide value in similar ways.
3. ==Word of mouth== communication exists between customers in the market, i.e. existing customers serve as high-value references for potential customers.

If these conditions aren't achieved, keep segmenting.

>[!Example]+ Smart Skin Care
>Smart Skin Care was the product of one of Aulet’s PhD students, Pedro Valencia. Valencia patented a new technology used to synthesize nanoparticles more quickly for medical uses, with one particular application being a polymer that binds to skin while slowly releasing medication over a twenty-four hour period. Valencia and his team spent weeks researching potential markets, working with hospitals, outpatient services, and cancer treatment centers. 
>
>During their search they discovered that one powerful application of their new technology was sunscreen—using time-release technology to slowly release sun-blocking chemicals over a period of time. 
>
>The sunscreen market was favorable because it ==required less time and resources== than medical markets monitored by the FDA. But the sunscreen market was still too large and diverse for the Smart Skin Care team, so they continued to sub-segment to find their beachhead market. 
>
>Finally, they discovered one of their sub-segments—**extreme athletes in their thirties who do triathlons**—had the interest, need, and disposable income to spend on an upgraded sunscreen. 
>
>After approaching their new beachhead market with success, Valencia and his team learned that if the extreme athletes bought their product, it would be that much easier to launch their product in other markets.

- motivations
- purchasing priorities

This helps in creating a ==shared understanding== of the customer and guides the company to take business decisions in line with customers interests.

### Total Addressable Market (TAM)
- **def:** The annual revenue generated from all the customers in the beachhead market (assuming you achieve 100% market share).
- **formula:** *total number of customers* in the beachhead market multiplied by the *revenue per customer*
- **goal:** have a big enough TAM to get a positive cash flow
- It's good to have an idea about the **compounded annual growth rate (CAGR)** of TAM.
### Realistic Market Share
- **SAM**
  Serviceable available market; the segment of the TAM targeted by your products and services which is within your geographical reach.
- **SOM**
  Serviceable obtainable market; the portion of SAM that you can capture.

## Market Research
### Benefits
By researching your target market and customers, you can:
- see if there’s a **demand** for your products and services
- determine the best branding, pricing and marketing strategies
- have research that supports your business idea to **convince investors**
### Primary Research
- gather *fresh* data
- answers questions relevant only to *your* company
- conducted by yourself, your team or by a market research firm hired for the purpose
- **data you collect belongs to you**, giving you an edge over your competitors
- the collected information is *up-to-date*, unlike secondary data which may be old and therefore no longer accurate
- specific results
#### Examples
- **Surveys**
  Ask the same questions to several people by email, phone, face-to-face, survey hosted on your company website, or using social media. 
  For example, you could email existing customers asking them to complete survey or use Facebook to get feedback on a new product or service idea you may have.
- **Focus Groups**
  Getting ~6-8 people in a room with a moderator to discuss a given subject and answer in more detail. 
  Often used by companies to get feedback on new product concepts, product packaging, pricing and marketing, but can be used to answer any primary research topic.
- **Interviews**
  One-to-one interviews may involve a questionnaire or a free-flowing discussion between the researcher and interviewee.
- **Observations**
  This is a qualitative method of collecting information either by *overtly* or *covertly* watching and observing others in natural or planned environments. 
  
  For example, an online retailer may set up a computer and observe how a test subject navigates their online store to purchase an item, with the aim of getting feedback that will help improve the online buying experience.

#### Why Companies Divide Populations into Market Segments?
1. To Determine Customer's Needs
   It's a basic truth that a company can only function if they have paying customers who are interested in their product or service. Hence, companies divide the population into market segments because these different market segments represent different customers with different needs. The company is essentially asking the question: "What does the customer want from us?" In doing so, the company is able to say with certainty who their products are meant for, and build consensus among their team.
   
   Mr.DIY, for instance, very clearly targets shoppers who are seeking convenience; that's their beachhead market. Back then, locals here in Malaysia will know that getting your items often meant going into multiple speciality stores. Maybe you'll need to go visit Uncle Muthu's hardware store here, or Chong's stationary store somewhere else, and it becomes a hassle to run around the neighbourhood, i.e. it becomes a pain point for customers. MR.DIY, through centralising many general products in one place, made things easy for shoppers, thus getting them paying customers.
   
   In short, Mr.DIY couldn't have achieved its current success without first evaluating that there was a market of customers who're willing to pay for the convenience they offered.
