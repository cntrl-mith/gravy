## Logic
- the study of arguments
- derived from greek *logos* meaning "reason", "discourse" and "statement", among others
- **argument:** contains statement(s) and conclusion
- **role of logic:** differentiate good and bad reasoning
- logics investigation is done through analysing the argument
## Methods of Thinking
### Deductive Reasoning
*specific* conclusions (always true) are drawn from *general* statements
```
Premise 1: All Human beings will die.
Premise 2: I am a Human Being.
Conclusion: I will die.
```
### Inductive Reasoning
*general* conclusions (probably true) are drawn from *specific* statements
``` 
Premise 1: 90% of UA students aren't proficient in English.
Premise 2: Zubaidah is a UA student.
Conclusion: Zubaidah may not be proficient in English.
```
### Abductive Reasoning
Think *backwards* on the cause & effects.
**Inferences** are made through predictions or (in)complete observations; you give the best explanation for a phenomenon that exists.
### Heuristics Reasoning
While [[#Inductive Reasoning|inductive]], [[#Deductive Reasoning|deductive]] and [[#Abductive Reasoning|abductive]] are ways of logical thinking where rule of thought can be applied, Heuristics is different as it has ==no applicable systematic or orderly rules==.

This method's **exploratory** in nature, but there are guidelines for implementation. Heuristics can be utilised through **trial and error** or "**lateral thinking**".
#### Availability Heuristics
- Describes the tendency to use information that *easily comes to mind* to inform decisions.
- rule of thumb/mental shortcut to guide our decisions, making deciding **easier**
- challenges the ability to to **accurately judge** the probability of certain events as memories are unreliable; some memories are *more prominent* due to emotional triggers
## Fallacy
Fallacy is a **mistake** in reasoning, where an argument does not support the claim.
*ex: tigers and lions are ruthless killers, so housecats must be ruthless killers.*
- **Ad Hominem**
  Occurs when acceptance or rejection of a concept is based on its *source* rather than its merit.
- **Appeal to Authority**
  Accepting a truth on blind faith because it's said by someone you *admire*.
- **Appeal to Pity**
  Someone seeks to gain acceptance by pointing out an *unfortunate consequence* that befalls them.
  *ex: if we don't adopt that puppy, they'll put him down, do we want to be responsible for that?* 
### Factors of Fallacies
- insufficient data and facts
- being prejudiced and pessimistic
- emotional stress
- distorted conclusions
- erroneous assumptions
- pressure/coercion
