## Metaphysics and Its Doctrine
Metaphysics concerns with the nature of **existence**, being and the world.

Aristotle believes that Metaphysics is the ==first philosophy== as it deals with the principles of **cause and effect**. The word is derived from Greek "metaphysika" (after physics), misinterpreted by medieval Commentator as "*above*" or "*beyond*" the physical.

Aristotle split it into 3 categories (with no clear order): 
- **Ontology**
  Study of being & existence, including definition and classification of entities, physical or mental nature of their properties and nature of change.
- **Natural Theology**
  Study of God, including nature of religion and the world, existence of the divine, questions about creation and various other religions or spiritual issues.
- **Universal Science**
  Study of first principles of logic and reasoning.
## Beliefs and the Relationship Between Man and God
### Metaphysics and Philosophy
**Philosophy** as a very abstract way or general study of events/phenomenons about nature. *ex: cause and effect*

For example, a chair has **physical attributes** like color, smell, feel. If those are removed, we are left with **substance** without specific qualities. Substance is the combination of mass & form of an object.
### Metaphysics and Religion/Belief
**Religions** about phenomenons beyond physical nature and cannot be proven rationally with empirical evidence. *ex: life after death, heaven, hell*

There are means of verification through **divine exposure** like Holy Books/the Quran. According to religion, reality isn't limited to physical attributes, and can contain other things like a soul that resides within the body, angels, genies.

Socialism assumes the philosophy of **materialism**, that the world is nothing more than material or nature, and all human endeavour are driven by the natural impulse of urge to live.
### Materialism
> "Matter is of primary importance as it shapes human beings, consciousness, and thought process." — Titus Lucretius Carus (99BC - 55BC)

- The dimension of ==human work== is the most important, rather than the spiritual. 
- Necessity is the cause of everything. 
- Anything that can be seen exists.
- Body & matter are the real existence, denying all spiritual matters.
- Idea comes from the brain.
### Idealistic Theory
- pioneered by George Berkeley (1685 - 1753)
- Approach to interpret idea or existence discussed in religion.
- Reality is perceived through ==sensation of object== which allows comprehension of beliefs and spirituality, like the existence of the soul.

### Theological Concepts (Vertical)
- **Theism**
  Belief in the existence of a god, even though that existence is invisible.
- **Atheism**
  Rejects the existence of God as only material things exist.
- **Monotheism**
  Belief in the existence of one god.
- **Polytheism**
  Belief in the existence of many gods.
- **Realism**
  Concern for fact or reality and rejection of the impractical and visionary.
- **Agnosticism**
  Existence of God is unknowable.
### Theological Concepts (Horizontal)
- **Hamka**
  Human is a *small universe* (Earth) & Universe is a *huge human* (Andromeda galaxy).
- **Determinism**
  Every single human action are based on cause & effect or determined laws which are intrinsic & extrinsic.
  Intrinsic: every incident happens because of your own will
  extrinsic: every incident happens because of external factors outside of human control
- **Realism**
  Which relates to Darwinism where the universe exists by itself.
- **Mechanism**
  Universe & Humans are like machines in which their behaviours have uniformity.
## Late 19th Century
Many science philosophers were uncomfortable with mystical and irrational thinking, rejecting anything that cannot be verified by experience. This approach was formally established by Moritz Schlick (1882 - 1936). Thus, Science began to replace metaphysics.

Auguste Comte (1798 - 1857) introduced philosophy of **Positivism** that is based on real facts and laws of science.
## Summary
Metaphysics is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of existence, being and the world.

It asks questions like: "What is the nature of reality?", "How does the world exist, and what is its origin or source of creation?", "Does the world exist outside the mind?", "How can the incorporeal mind affect the physical body?", "If things exist, what is their objective nature?", "Is there a God (or many gods, or no god at all)?"
