## Theory and Condition of Knowledge
> "Knowledge is justified as true belief."—Al-karsawi, 2018 

Knowledge refers to human **understanding** of something, which is a *systematic* and consciously worked understanding. Knowledge involves three conditions:
- **Truth**
  Knowledge must be true.
- **Belief**
  Not believing in something is seen as having no knowledge of it.
- **Justification**
  Knowledge must be justified; assumptions and gusses are bad justifications.
### Why We Need Knowledge
- knowledge is both a **tool** and **goal** of humanity
- understand the reality of the world
- discover the function, role and justification for man's existence
## Epistemology
- branch of philosophy investigating the origin, nature, methods, and limits of human knowledge
- comes from the Greek words:
	- *episteme:* knowledge or understanding or acquaintance
	- *logos:* account or argument or reason
- *al-llm* in Arabic
### History of Epistemology
1. **Ancient Greece**
   Science is *theoretical* and *speculative* and not based on experimentation.
2. **Empirical Era**
   Empirical evidence is needed for arguments (intellectual, rational, empirical), leading to advancements of science and technology.
3. **Christianity**
   Separation of science and religion and value-free concepts/neutral stances, leading to different views and perspectives on issues in knowledge.
### Epistemology's Main Questions
- What is the reality of knowledge?
- What are the sources to acquire knowledge?
- Can man have boundless knowledge about everything or is there a limit to it?
> (Zaki, 2018; Al-Karsawi, 2018)

## Theories on Truth
### Correspondent
- Statement is true if it matches what exists in *real life*.
- first proposed by **Plato** and **Aristotle** vaguely in metaphysics
- Most popular.
### Coherent
- Statement is true if it is *consistent* with other statements.
- **advocators:** George Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel & Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
- Helps in revealing how we **arrive at truth** as we work to fit our beliefs into a coherent system.
### Pragmatic
- Statement is true if it is *useful* to believe and *practical*. Impractical knowledge are illusions.
- **advocator:** Charles Sander Peirce
## Epistemology for Daily Use
Epistemology is the explanation of how a person **thinks**, which helps differentiate between truth and lies. Epistemology makes use of 4 **sources** for gathering knowledge:
1. Intuitive
2. Authoritative
3. Logical
4. Empirical
### Examples of Epistemology in Real Life
- Security Issues
- Scientific Discovery
- Legal Affairs
- Decision Making
- Critical Thinking
> [!Example] Crime Scene
> A crime scene needs a detailed investigation and a brief overview of *similar cases* to impart new information and produce a **justification** for the knowledge.
## Summary
1. Epistemology plays a key role in *comprehending* the world that we live in.
2. The absence of epistemology would mean there's no **explanation** of how various phenomena existing in nature works.
3. Epistemology helps the philosophers **construct rational theories** that support and justify various questions such as:
	- How do we know that the knowledge possessed by mankind regarding our planet to date is true?
	- What is the difference between knowledge and belief?
	- Will the world be a better place in the absence of caste and religion?
	- What is intelligence?
4. Although some of the answers to various aspects of the world are still unknown to mankind, epistemology ensures the scope to develop an **appropriate response** to some extent.
