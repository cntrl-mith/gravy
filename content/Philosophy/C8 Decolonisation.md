## Definition of Decolonisation
- the transition from a world of colonial empires to a world of **nation-states** in the years after WW2
- The Netherlands, France, and Belgium were defeated and the UK severely depleted, leading the people under colonial rule to rise and break the ties binding them to Europe, marking the emergence of decolonisation.
### Colonisation vs Decolonisation
Colonisation means a society is subject to colonialism whether from the physical, material, mental, & intellectual aspects. The West (US, France, Great Britain etc.) has influenced the world in this way.

Decolonisation is a *derivative* term of colonial, used to mean freeing ourselves from colonial influence (politics, economy, social etc). This is because society was **exposed** to knowledge of modernisation according to Western molds. 
### Neo-Colonialism
Neo-colonialism is when the former colonisers, even after leaving, have a **direct influence** to free and independent nations. It was first introduced by **Kwame Nkrumah**, who was the leader of Ghana as it became independent, in his book "Neo-colonialism: The Last Stage of Imperialism".

While actual colonisation has ended, the *influence* of colonising powers still remains. In some ways, it is more insidious as it is **harder to discern**. 

For example, the *economic* influence left by the colonial power still played a role, and the government has people whose *sympathies* aligned with the colonisers (usually the native elite trained by colonisers). The term is especially pertinent to African leaders.

In the later stages post-1980s, and even after the fall of the Soviet Union, the **US** and its influence (both economic and political) in the world is seen as the main influence in this new colonial regime. The US achieves this through **direct invasion** of countries like Iraq, Iran etc, and through its **economic policy**, coercing smaller nations to to bend to its will and enticing new governments to follow its policy.

## Decolonisation of Ideology: Cartesian Dualism
Cartesian Dualism was proposed by René Descartes, a French mathematician and philosopher, in the 17th century. It suggests there are fundamentally two kinds of stuff:

- **matter stuff/physical essence**, which follows the laws of physics
  *ex: planets, rocks*
- **mind stuff/spiritual essence**, which doesn't
  *ex: *human consciousness, thought*

It models the mind as being *attached* to but *separate* from the body, and although mind stuff and matter stuff can affect each other, they're not the same thing. 

In the modern era, a different suggestion is that the mind is an **emergent** property. 

Think of how snowflakes are formed simply because water molecules follow the laws of chemistry, rather than some innate desire for beauty. It's still *intrinsically tied* to the nature of the molecule but not something that's *contained* within the molecules.

Similarly, neurons don't contain the mind; the mind is just what happens when you get a shit ton of neurons talking to each other.
