## Story
1. System owner receives complaint.
2. System analyst does initial study of problems with the current system.
3. Analyst creates a **proposal** with a list of problems, their solutions, the cost and time needed for implementation
   The solution is usually a refinement or a replacement of the system.
4. Owner can decide to accept or reject the proposal.
5. On acceptance, the analyst must:
6. appoint developers, hired through a **tender system/process**.
7. the developer starts building the application
### Tender Process
Implemented by most businesses. Can be skipped by having **in-house developers**, but unnecessary if the company doesn't need full-time developers.

The system owner wants to implement the system in a *cheap*, *fast* way that promises *good quality*. So they create a **specification** of what they want. The developers receive the specifications, to confirm that they're able to create it. Devs can also be found via advertising.

The developers will conduct a **feasibility study** to find out if they can complete the offer within the budget and timeline, coming up with their own **proposal**. For example, if the budget is 50k and must be done in 4 weeks, vendor A might say they can do it for less than 40k but take 5 weeks. whereas vendor B can do it in 2 weeks but for 60k.

Now negotiations happen. Corruption can happen here. After a vendor is selected, a contract is signed (a MOU, memorandum of understanding). 
## Information System Methodologies
SDLC is a general framework, whereas methodologies are *specific* frameworks that are custom to help create specific types of systems.

There are two types of methodologies:
1. **Structured** 
	- 70s-90s
	- waterfall model (still used)
	- SSADM (obsolete)
	- V Model (obsolete)
2. **Agile** 
	- Agile it not *itself* a methodology, though many websites may claim this, they're actually talking about scrum.
	- Early 90s, IT industry expanded, traditional doesn't have any principles, 17 people in Utah sat down and made the *Agile Manifesto*, the principles of Agile methods.
	- methods: (often called agile umbrella)
		- scrum
		- XP
		- RAD
		- DSDM
		- Lean
		- Kanban
		- Spiral
		- RUP (obsolete)
	- think of these as for creating *specialised* types of software
	- See [[C5 Agile Methodologies]] for agile principles
