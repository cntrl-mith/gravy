# Agile Principles

There are 12 Agile Principles, taken from the *Agile Manifesto*.
### 1. Achieve Customer satisfaction by early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
- *valuable* means functional software.
- *early* means ahead of competitors.
- *continuous* means keep delivering systems of increasing functionality every two weeks.
- *customer satisfaction* means focus only on what the customer actually wants.

> [!Example] Nokia and Apple
> Early on, Apple and Nokia would release their products and declare "these are the best you'll get from us sayonara" rather than continuously iterating upon them.
> 
> Nowadays, modern apple phones have versions upon versions coming out every few months, making it clear that the original approach was a mistake.
### 2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in the development.
- Customers are always changing their minds, developers must adapt and expect this by keeping their structures flexible.
- Of course, we should charge extra for changes to keep frivolous changes to a minimum.
### 3. Working software is delivered frequently.
- Smaller, frequent progress updates are always better than big, slow updates.

>[!Example] Angry Birds Developer
>If you're paid to develop 10 levels of angry birds, and you deliver those 10 levels at the end of 10 months, that doesn't leave room for adjustment.
>
>Instead, delivering a level is far better, allowing the customer to give feedback and conduct more testing.

### 4. Close, daily cooperation between business-people and developers.
- *business-people* here refers to the internal staff or other users of a system that isn't the customer.
- This one was written because early on, developers tended to focus on customer needs while neglecting business-people.
>[!Example] APU Website
>Take APU's website for example, we have **customers** (students & other people) who use the website to find out things like *timetable*, *course details*, *intake dates*.
>
>But APU also caters to the business-people consisting of *lecturers*, *admin* etc, who have different needs like giving students their marks.
### 5. Projects are built around motivated individuals, who should be trusted.
- Developers should break away from **traditional office structure**; individuals should be motivated to innovate.
- Reward employees for contributing ideas, not just doing work as they're told.
### 6. Face-to-face or Real Time Conversation is the best form of communication. (Co-location)
- The *default* should always be conversation. Written specifications or plans should be created only if need be.
### 7. software is the principal measure of progress.
- Progress is defined only by working software, the quantity of documentation or the current phase is irrelevant.
### 8. Ensure Sustainable development, maintain a constant pace.
- Instead of going at full speed and burning out, go at a consistent pace.
- Project managers should divide work **equally** among employees instead of overloading one good one.
- Can also be interpreted as ensuring software is *ethical*, environment is unharmed etc etc.
### 9. Pay Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design.
- Maintain the highest quality of work.
### 10. Simplicity, the art of maximising the amount of work not done, is essential.
- Do only the necessary things, as fast as possible, in as short a time as possible, as simple as possible.
### 11. Self-organising teams produce the best architectures, requirements, and design.
- self-organising teams are teams that manage the responsibilities passed to them **amongst themselves**, instead of being micromanaged
### 12. Regularly, the team reflects on how to become more effective, and adjusts accordingly.
- After everything, sit down and discuss lessons learnt.
- This part is important but often neglected.
